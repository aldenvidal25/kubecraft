Exercise 5 Deliverables: Why /30 at .200 breaks reaching .1

host1's eth1 was set to 10.1.1.200/30.

A /30 mask only covers 4 addresses: 10.1.1.200 - 10.1.1.203.
- Network address:   10.1.1.200
- Broadcast address: 10.1.1.203
- Usable hosts:      10.1.1.201 - 10.1.1.202

srl1's address, 10.1.1.1, falls way outside that range (200-203).

How a host decides "local" vs "needs a gateway":
A host ANDs the destination IP with its own subnet mask. If the result
matches its own network, the destination is considered local (same
subnet, reachable directly via ARP). If not, the host must route the
packet out through a gateway.

With a /30 mask, 10.1.1.1 ANDed against 255.255.255.252 does NOT match
host1's own network (10.1.1.200/30). So host1 thinks .1 is NOT on its
local subnet, even though both addresses start with "10.1.1." and look
close together. Since no gateway is configured for this mismatched
subnet, the packet has nowhere to go, producing "Network is unreachable."

Lesson: the subnet mask -- not how close two IPs look numerically --
defines the boundary of a local network. A small mask like /30 creates
a tiny 4-address island; addresses just outside that island are treated
as remote, even if they're "next door" by IP number.
